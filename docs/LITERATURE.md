# Literature and what is proposed here

Checked against primary sources on 4 September 2026. This is a targeted review, not a guarantee that no closely related work exists. Recheck before manuscript submission. Sources below support their stated findings; model choices and deliverables in this repository are project proposals/implementations, not statements made by those sources.

1. LVK, *GWTC-4.0: Population Properties of Merging Compact Binaries*, final ApJL publication (2026), Appendix D.7 and Figure 23. A model fitted to GWTC-3 is used to predict O4a detected cumulative distributions. This is direct precedent for the chronological split. Consult the final publication because versions can differ: https://inspirehep.net/files/78c731c70f47db811fd8178af88cbd3c ; arXiv record https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18083 .
2. LVK, *GWTC-5.0: Population Properties of Merging Compact Binaries* (2026), arXiv:2605.27226, version 2 dated 1 July. The population analysis through O4b discusses the lower-mass feature and high-mass slope change, with reduced support for a local high-mass peak. This motivates a comparative predictive feature audit rather than claiming novelty for noticing weaker peak support. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27226 .
3. Corelli et al., *The Variance of Gravitational-Wave Populations* (2026), arXiv:2603.00239. Resampling catalog/observing segments examines feature stability under catalog realizations. It is related to, but not identical with, chronological prediction or numerical Monte Carlo error. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00239 .
4. Miller et al., *Posterior Predictive Checks for Gravitational-wave Populations: Limitations and Improvements* (2026), arXiv:2604.06090. Predictive checks can be insensitive when event parameters are weakly constrained; spin examples motivate explicit discriminatory-power checks, not abandonment of predictive inference. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06090 .
5. Mandel, Farr & Gair, *Extracting distribution parameters from multiple uncertain observations with selection biases* (2019), arXiv:1809.02063. Hierarchical inference requires consistent measurement uncertainty and selection normalization. https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02063 .
6. Talbot & Golomb, *Growing Pains: Understanding the Impact of Likelihood Uncertainty on Hierarchical Bayesian Inference for Gravitational-Wave Astronomy* (2023), arXiv:2304.06138. Finite Monte Carlo likelihood estimates can matter increasingly as catalog sizes grow. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06138 .
7. *Comparing astrophysical models to gravitational-wave data in observable space* (2025), arXiv:2507.13249. Relevant adjacent model-comparison work; compare the final proposed claim and observable-space treatment against it before claiming a new method. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13249 .
8. GWOSC GWTC-5.0 release documentation: event, parameter-estimation and search-sensitivity product entry points, including updated O4a products. Verify exact record versions and checksums locally. https://gwosc.org/GWTC-5.0/ .
9. Optional inference software: Bilby hyperparameter inference https://bilby-dev.github.io/bilby/hyperparameters.html ; GWPopulation documentation https://colmtalbot.github.io/gwpopulation/ . Inherited package pins are not assertions about current latest versions or scientific validation.

## Project inference, not a literature finding

The potentially useful contribution is a matched, uncertainty-aware external-prediction comparison with demonstrable power, failure diagnostics and reusable reference products. A second methods paper would require a distinct general result, such as a qualified detectability boundary under measurement/selection uncertainty, not repackaging the first paper's validation section. Neither novelty, journal placement nor citations are guaranteed.
